# Schoolyard art slice (issue #82)

The first hand-made visual slice of the schoolyard (`scenes/locations/playground/`).
It covers the visual layer only. Colliders, navigation, interactables, exits and spawns
are the graybox's and are not touched. `tests/playground_art_slice_test.gd` guards that.

## Provenance

Everything in this folder is the project's own work. Nothing was downloaded, traced,
photographed or generated by an image model, and no third-party asset or licence is
involved.

| Files | Made by | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `textures/*.png` (25) | `source/paint_playground_textures.py` | Procedural painting from fixed seeds (2101–2125), using the classroom painter's brush vocabulary (`art/classroom/paint_classroom_textures.py`, imported read-only). It is deterministic: the same command on Pillow 11.3 / NumPy 2.2 repaints byte-identical files. |
| `textures/*.png.import` | same script | Lossless, mipmapped, VRAM detection off (same settings as the classroom textures). |
| `materials/*.tres` (47) | `source/build_playground_art.py` | Toon `StandardMaterial3D` (`diffuse_mode = 3`, `specular_mode = 2`) plus one shared ink outline pass. |
| `scenes/locations/playground/playground.tscn` (art pass) | `source/build_playground_art.py` | Applied on top of the graybox written by `tools/build_outdoor_locations.py`. |

The issue's file allowlist does not include `docs/ASSET_PROVENANCE.md`, so the record
lives here. A one-line pointer to this README can be added there when the docs are next
touched.

## Rebuilding

```sh
python3 -m venv /tmp/paint && /tmp/paint/bin/pip install pillow==11.3.0 numpy==2.2.6
/tmp/paint/bin/python scenes/environment/props/playground_art/source/paint_playground_textures.py
# Only after the graybox changed: regenerate it first, then re-apply the art pass.
python3 tools/build_outdoor_locations.py
/tmp/paint/bin/python scenes/environment/props/playground_art/source/build_playground_art.py
```

`build_playground_art.py` refuses to run on a scene that already has the pass
(`PlaygroundArt` node), so it can never be applied twice. It also fails on two
conditions:

- a graybox mesh is left on a shared `assets/materials/` material;
- a material is written but never used.

## Composition

The yard is a stage that is waiting, so its middle stays empty: it is kept for people
and events (`docs/IMPLEMENTATION_NOTES_PLAYGROUND.md`).

- **Sky and distance.** The game camera looks down at about 53° from the south-east, so
  past the open west edge it sees *below* the horizon. The dusk sky is laid flat under
  the yard as a painted floor (`sky_floor.png`), in bands by distance from the yard edge:
  pale glow, rose, mauve, then dusty blue. Four flat cut-out ground rows stand on it at
  x ≤ −25 m:
  - flats
  - water tower
  - chimney
  - pylon and trees
  - All are unlit, cast no shadows and never sit between the camera and the player.
- **Ground.** Two pours of mid-value concrete. The older pour is warm sand; the newer
  pour, east of the seam, is a cooler grey. The seam crosses the yard and comes up
  through the court paint. The near hoop stands in a cooler patch of newer concrete with
  four filled bolt holes.
- **Court.** Oxide-red paint with hand-brushed bone lines and dusty-blue keys (one key
  outline does not quite close). The far half is darker: it was painted later. In the
  graybox, `CourtFarHalf` sat 1 mm *under* the court surface; it now sits above it.
- **A few isolated colour accents**, each on something the player is meant to find:
  - the yellow climbing frame and hoop rings
  - the green shed door, with its hasp and brass padlock
  - the red fire door (the way back into the school)
  - the ochre block under the bench and the bag
  - Everything else is big quiet planes:
    - school wall: sage teal
    - shed: ivory
    - container: muted blue
    - estate blocks: plum grey
- **Hand marks.** Wobbling ink chain-link (the gap in the north-west run is cut into the
  texture at x = −14), brushed court lines, rain streaks on the backboards, and a pale
  brush gesture on the container. There is also the duty sheet in the shed window, with
  unreadable entries and the last row written twice, and a fresher painted patch on the
  school wall where something used to hang.

Lighting (in `playground.tscn`):

- Sun: same bearing as the graybox (from behind the camera), raised from 12° to 20°.
  The south estate block's dusk shadow is now a deliberate cool block over roughly the
  gate half of the yard instead of all of it. Colour `(1, 0.8, 0.62)`, energy 0.32
  (measured from the CI frames: at 0.85 the sunlit ground clipped to white).
- Ambient: cool `(0.66, 0.64, 0.74)` × 0.8, so shadows go mauve rather than brown.
- Filmic tonemap, exposure 0.62. Measured from the CI frames at exposure 1.0: the
  shadowed ground sat at about sRGB 127 and the sunlit ground at 215 to 250, so the
  pale albedos had no headroom. One exposure value scales everything evenly and keeps
  the authored palette ratios intact.
- Background: the sky blue.
- The surroundings stay dim, as around a lit stage. Every sky band stays mid-to-dark,
  and the sunlit ground stays mid-value: the HUD line (top left) and the interaction prompts (bottom centre) are pale text and sit exactly
  over them.

## Triplanar conventions

These matter if you touch the materials. With `uv1_triplanar`, Godot samples:

- the top face at `(x', −z')`
- the ±Z faces at `(x', −y')`
- the ±X faces at `(−z', −y')`

where `p' = p * uv1_scale + uv1_offset`.

That is why the fence materials use `scale.y = 1 / height`, which puts the bottom rail
on the ground. `fence_gap` uses `offset.x = 19.5 / 11`, so the cut lands at x = −14.
The sheets use local triplanar, so one material fits both hoop boards.
