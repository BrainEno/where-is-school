# Schoolyard art slice (issue #82)

The first hand-made visual slice of the schoolyard (`scenes/locations/playground/`).
The art pass leaves the graybox route and collision layout in place, except for the
hollow container and its roof-to-ground ladder documented below. The bench keeps its
collider; the noodle film replaces a backpack whose box was already inside that collider. `tests/playground_art_slice_test.gd`
guards the surrounding yard.

## Provenance

Everything in this folder is the project's own work. Nothing was downloaded, traced,
photographed or generated by an image model, and no third-party asset or licence is
involved.

| Files | Made by | How |

The fictional print on the films, paper and sign is drawn with the existing Noto Sans SC source. It is deterministic: the same command on Pillow 11.3 / NumPy 2.2 repaints byte-identical files. |

## Rebuilding

Use the stable Godot editor from `.godot-version` (`--engine /path/to/godot` if not on
`PATH`). The baker uses a temporary project; it does not modify the main import cache.

```sh
python3 -m venv /tmp/paint && /tmp/paint/bin/pip install pillow==11.3.0 numpy==2.2.6
/tmp/paint/bin/python scenes/environment/props/playground_art/source/paint_playground_textures.py
# To rebuild the yard, regenerate the graybox first, then re-apply the art pass.
python3 tools/build_outdoor_locations.py playground
/tmp/paint/bin/python scenes/environment/props/playground_art/source/build_playground_art.py
```

`build_playground_art.py` refuses to run on a scene that already has the pass
(`PlaygroundArt` node), so it can never be applied twice. The render-only
`PlaygroundArt` branch is saved as the compressed `scenery.scn` instance. Fittings
attached to occluding bodies stay in the location scene, so they still fade with
those bodies. The node paths, physics, interactables and camera rig do not change.

The Python sources own the scenery geometry and colours; do not edit the binary
bakes directly. The temporary OBJ imports and private scenery material files are
not committed or exported. Only the triplanar site meshes omit UVs and redundant
shadow meshes; the production surface positions, triangles and normal data are
kept. `--materials-only` rewrites just the shared `.tres` palette and does not bake
or require an editor.

The script also fails if a graybox mesh retains a shared `assets/materials/`
material, a generated material goes unused, or a scenery-only override is also
referenced by one of the playable yard fittings or props.

## Roof visit and resident

`RoofEncounter` is a sibling of the container at its origin, so it is not part of
`scenery.scn` and does not fade with the shell. On either window route, roof contact
wakes the resident on the existing sheet. After the landing pose the door shot
follows his rise and walk across the threshold. No furniture or collisions are
added; the ladder and the existing inspections still use the same nodes.

Rebuild the native figure with `python3 tools/build_roof_collector.py` (or use
`--check` to check its output). Its profile and paint data are in the builder;
`roof_collector_pose.gd` moves the joints, and `roof_encounter.tscn` owns the bed,
route and camera marks. There is no external model, image or animation to import.

A hard landing changes the live 3D view into a slate and vermilion misprint:
ink density bands, angled halftone dots, paper tooth and a second, slightly
misregistered red plate. This adapts the medium change of [#196](https://github.com/BrainEno/where-is-school/pull/196)
and paper/red separation of [#200](https://github.com/BrainEno/where-is-school/pull/200),
not their experimental scenes. The print starts at contact on the hard route
only, draws behind the UI and outlines, and ends when you leave the yard. Continue
on the same roof visit restores the final state, without replaying the impact or
the walk. The ordinary entrances never apply the print.

`Classroom anomaly capture` records the two landings, both door shots and the
same court angle on both routes. The playable state and Continue are covered by
`tests/classroom_anomaly_test.gd`; art approval still depends on seeing the motion.
The exchange with the resident is [pending the author's text](../../../../data/dialogue_schema/README.md#container-resident--lines-and-choices-not-yet-in-the-game).

## Composition

The yard is a stage that is waiting, so its middle stays empty: it is kept for people
and events (`docs/IMPLEMENTATION_NOTES_PLAYGROUND.md`).

- **Sky and distance.** The game camera looks down at about 53° from the south-east, so
  past the open west edge it sees *below* the horizon. The dusk sky is laid flat under
  the yard as a painted floor (`sky_floor.png`), in bands by distance from the yard edge:
  dim glow, rose, mauve, then dusty blue. Four flat cut-out ground rows stand on it at
  x ≤ −25 m:
  - flats
  - water tower
  - chimney
  - pylon and trees
  - All are unlit, cast no shadows and never sit between the camera and the player.
- **The building under construction** (`PlaygroundArt/BuildingSite`, the instanced
  `construction_site.tscn`, written by `source/construction_site.py`). The plot meets the
  yard at the same edge; it is a backdrop, not a walkable extension. Round 9 rebuilt every
  part as generated geometry (about 68k triangles) instead of primitives and lofts:
  - The ellipsoid building has a closed, opaque, 360° concrete shell with recessed cold
    joints, pour-to-pour tone, rain streaks under the deck and splashback at the foot, a
    grid of formwork tie holes and two downpipes on stand-off clamps. The deck carries
    timber formwork, yellow edge protection with toe boards, starter bars, chalk and the six
    marbles. A plywood porch with a steel door, a vision panel, a site board and a lamp
    faces the gate: the future way in.
  - Three tipped sand piles with slumped, rilled flanks, a bitten face where the shovel
    went in, pebbles and a damp skirt. A smaller spoil mound, a wheelbarrow, a rebar bundle
    and a pallet of blocks sit inside.
  - Trapezoid-ribbed hoarding panels (three variants) with mud splash and rust runs, posts,
    ballast blocks, couplers and a pale capping rail. The gate has two barred leaves on
    hinge posts, hazard boards, a blank name board, a chain round both stiles and a brass
    padlock; one panel lies fallen inside the south run.
  - The excavator keeps its reach over the fence: tracks with shoes, rollers and idlers,
    a counterweighted house, glazed cab, boom and stick with hydraulic cylinders and hoses,
    and a toothed bucket holding sand.
  - The ground has machine ruts, sand spill and puddles, flattened to the yard's level along
    the shared edge.
  - All geometry is at x ≤ −23.05 m (the walkable yard ends near −22.5) and the script
    refuses to write anything past it; no collision or navigation. `SiteLook` (an
    inspection at the yard edge in front of the gate) carries the `building_site` camera
    shot, which backs the camera off and lifts its aim (`CameraShot.look_height`) so the
    whole building is in frame.
- **Ground.** Two pours of low-value concrete: the stage floor stays dark so the court,
  the props and the pale HUD text carry the light. The older pour is warm sand; the newer
  pour, east of the seam, is a cooler grey. The seam crosses the yard and comes up
  through the court paint. The near hoop stands in a cooler patch of newer concrete with
  four filled bolt holes.
- **Court.** Oxide-red paint with hand-brushed bone lines and dusty-blue keys (one key
  outline, in a dimmer bone, does not quite close). The far half is darker: it was painted later. In the
  graybox, `CourtFarHalf` sat 1 mm *under* the court surface; it now sits above it.
- **A few isolated colour accents**, each on something the player is meant to find:
  - the yellow climbing frame and hoop rings
  - the green shed door, with its hasp and brass padlock
  - the red fire door (the way back into the school)
  - split cream-and-red noodle film on the wooden bench; the ochre block under it stays dim
  - Everything else is big quiet planes:
    - school wall: sage teal
    - shed: ivory
    - container: muted blue
    - estate blocks: plum grey
- **Hand marks.** Wobbling ink chain-link (the gap in the north-west run is cut into the
  texture at x = −14), brushed court lines, rain streaks on the backboards, and a pale
  brush gesture on the container. There is also the duty sheet in the shed window, with
  unreadable entries and the last row written twice, and a fresher painted patch on the
  school wall where something used to hang.

### HUD readability

The top-left sentence (`ObjectiveLabel`, sRGB about `#C6BBA4`) and the interaction prompt
(`PromptLabel`, `#DBD4BF`) are pale text with no outline, drawn by the global UI, which
this slice may not change. The prompt sits over the ground just in front of the player,
wherever the player is; the sentence sits over whatever lies about 15 m north-west of them.
So the floor and the sky are kept dark enough for both:

| Surface (sunlit, brightest tenth) | Estimated on screen | Prompt | Sentence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ground / newer pour / hoop patch | L 0.077 / 0.068 / 0.061 | 5.6 / 6.0 / 6.4 | 4.4 / 4.7 / 5.0 |
| Court / keys / bench block | L 0.094 / 0.095 / 0.089 | 4.9 / 4.9 / 5.1 | 3.8 / 3.8 / 4.0 |
| Sky floor (unlit) | L 0.078 | 5.5 | 4.3 |

Contrast is the WCAG ratio; the targets are 4.5 for the prompt and 3.5 for the sentence.
The estimate is albedo × (ambient + toon-lit Sun) × exposure through Godot's Filmic curve.
Against the CI frames of three lighting rounds it matched the measured ground within
4 sRGB levels. `tests/playground_art_slice_test.gd` (claim 10) runs it from every standing
spot with the real camera: it casts rays through the text positions, checks for shadow
towards the Sun, and fails if the floor or the sky behind the text drops below the target.

Not covered: the sunlit faces of the pale standing things. These are the ivory shed walls,
the plinth and the south face of the school wall, at roughly 1.2–2.5 against the
sentence. The test reports them but does not fail on them. Darkening them as well would
put the whole yard in murk and lose the few lit objects the composition is built on. A text
shadow or backing on the HUD would solve it everywhere, but that belongs to the UI, not to
this slice.

## Triplanar conventions

These matter if you touch the materials. With `uv1_triplanar`, Godot samples:

- the top face at `(x', −z')`
- the ±Z faces at `(x', −y')`
- the ±X faces at `(−z', −y')`

where `p' = p * uv1_scale + uv1_offset`.

That is why the fence materials use `scale.y = 1 / height`, which puts the bottom rail
on the ground. `fence_gap` uses `offset.x = 19.5 / 11`, so the cut lands at x = −14.
The sheets use local triplanar, so one material fits both hoop boards.

### Roof, ladder and cabin

`container.tscn` is generated by `source/build_playground_art.py` from Godot primitives
and the UV meshes in `meshes/`, with separate plates for the 6 × 2.4 m hollow shell, two
open leaves, a threshold and an unchanged roof spawn (`FromSchool`, 2.6 m above grade).
The rods, hinges, frayed-looking sheet, pushed-aside blanket and overlapping newsprint
are actual geometry. There is no cot, mattress, pillow or table: this is bedding on the
0.22 m deck, with a clear strip toward the opening. Roof corrugation remains decorative
above the continuous landing surface.

The prologue has two ways onto the same roof (braced or injured). The **roof-side** ladder
prompt turns the character to the rails and stages eight levels, from the top rung down to
the yard. It is not a ground-to-roof navigation link. The rung marks and the pose track
share the same beat clock; two-joint corrections keep the boots on alternating treads and
the wrists at the rails during support. The save point records the player's ground
transform after the beat, not a mid-ladder pose. From the base, walk around the right-hand end to see the
interior; activating the inspection also lowers the camera to look through the doorway.
The cut newspaper optionally records a separate observation. It adds no gate, item pickup,
actor identity or effect on the existing destination/choice checks.

### Playable yard fittings

The standpipe, cast outlet, red wheel, hanging cap and slotted drain describe the water
path without adding a water simulation. The equipment shed has a framed door with an
uneven hasp, a shut glazed window with the original rota and return copy, and a folded
roof edge and downpipe. The wall fittings fade with `ShedBody`. The teaching-wing view
uses the same `ShedRoof` slab, without the yard's raised seams and gutter.
The door remains the same dialogue/check, not a walkable interior. The scene keeps the
old court paint, cracks and empty centre.
`tests/capture_classroom_anomalies.gd` photographs the doorway and authored prop focus
shots from the in-game rig (not a free camera) as part of its existing Actions workflow.

Regenerate just this yard with:

```sh
python3 tools/build_outdoor_locations.py playground
python3 scenes/environment/props/playground_art/source/build_playground_art.py
```
