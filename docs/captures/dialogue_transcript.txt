Q: She's got third period on the timetable, so. She'll be in at the bell.

BEAT D — INSPECTIONS (content as shown in the result card)
========================================================================
[schoolbag] Schoolbag
    Under the chair, strap first, the way you leave it when you are already late. Inside: a pencil case, a folded handout, a bus card with the corner chewed. Everything you would have brought. Everything except the biology textbook.

[own_desk] Your desk
    Varnish worn to the wood in a hand-shaped patch. A previous occupant has scratched a date into the corner and then scratched it out. Your handwriting would fit the same size of space.

[set_square] Wooden set square
    A wooden right angle, older than the desks, with a split running up the short leg. Someone has sanded the split instead of throwing the thing away, and sanded it badly. It sits on the teacher's desk at an angle that could only be deliberate.

[teacher_desk] The teacher's desk
    Two meters of dark wood, set square and square to the room. One drift of chalk dust that nobody has wiped. The exercise books are stacked on the far corner, all facing out, spines aligned. Nothing on this desk is for the person who sits behind it.

[chalkboard] Chalkboard
    BIOLOGY, THIRD PERIOD — written in a square, unhurried hand. Under it, in a different colour, TERM BEGINS. Both look written on the same day. Somebody has erased the middle of the board and given up.

[timetable] Timetable
    One sheet in a plastic sleeve by the door: this class, this term. Biology is third period most days. Thursday has been crossed out in pen and written back in with a different pen. Nobody has initialled it.

[window] Window
    The glass has a grey bloom on the inside you can rub off with a thumb. Below: a strip of wet asphalt, the side of a skip, the tops of two heads going nowhere. Beyond the boundary wall the roofs are ordinary and close together.

    [choices]
      1. （In your head: “I don't remember Thursday.”)
      2. （In your head: “I was here. I think I was here.”)
      3. （In your head: nothing at all. Just the desk.)
    > you choose: （In your head: “I don't remember Thursday.”)
B (imagined): Then we'll do this the long way. Textbook out.
    In the rehearsal you have no bag. You have not had one the whole time.
    Then, in the rehearsal, the bell goes early. Which is not how bells work.
    You stop. You can hear the yard again — someone moving a bucket along concrete.

BEAT F — THE BELL, THEN THE SUBSTITUTE
========================================================================
(the bell rings twice; the chair at the teacher's desk stays empty)
    The door opens at a slightly wrong moment — too early for a teacher, too late for a student.
N: Sorry. Sorry. Third period? Biology, third period?
N: I'm not — she's not in today. I'm covering. There should be a sheet.
    The sheet is not where she left it. N looks at the desk the way you look at a drawer you did not open.
N: It's usually. Under the. Okay. We can start without it.
    [choices]
      1. “Is she coming in later?”
      2. “Do we need the textbook for this?”
      3. （Wait for her to find the sheet.)
    > you choose: “Do we need the textbook for this?”
N: Um. Yes. Probably. Page forty-something.
N: I don't have a copy. Which is. Not ideal. I know.
    She finds the sheet halfway through the folder, under something else.
N: This is — sorry, this is a seating plan. There's two names crossed out.
N: I'm not going to sort out the seating plan on my first day. Let's just.
N: Look. If the rest of you are going to do something that isn't this, then do it quietly. I have to log the period either way.
N: You can be out in the corridor, or in the yard. Not off the site. Not yet.
N: Actually — I'd better come with you for the corridor. Later. In a minute.
    The door stays open. Nobody gets up immediately. Then somebody does, and then it's a room that is allowed to leave.
    N sits down at the teacher's desk, then stands up again, then sits down.
